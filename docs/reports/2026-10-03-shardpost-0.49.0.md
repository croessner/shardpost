# ShardPost 0.49.0: separate full-text caps (stage B14g)

Date: 2026-10-03

## Change

First code stage of the plan for the full-text dictionary of large
accounts (`temp/13-fts-dictionary-large-accounts.md`, Fassung 4, after
two design review rounds).

- **Separate caps and targets:** `fts.shards_max` (default now 128)
  bounds postings and heads; the dictionary has its own
  `fts.dict_shards_max` (512) and `fts.dict_target_keys` (100000). The
  write-path trigger stays silent at a cap; `plan -due-only` reports
  `capped`; `imapceph_fts_shards_capped_total` has a `kind` label.
- **fts_due worker:** the no-op loop is gone. After a no-op job the wait
  doubles (6 h up to 7 days) and the entry is removed; a writer queues
  the account again when its trigger fires.
- **Faster conversions:** the copy phase of `fts_layout` writes with
  `fts.convert_writers` (8) in parallel, with a bounded buffer
  (`fts.convert_buffer_keys`) and a fence before every write.
- **`shardpostctl fts stats`:** key counts per object from the counters
  (`-exact` scans, throttled); counts by token class only for accounts in
  `fts.stats_test_accounts`; no term ever leaves the server.
- **Shape parameter:** `plan -shards s/d/h` beside `-shards n`.
- **Bench:** corpus variants `ids` and `ids,nosig`; `shardpost-load
  -corpus … -rcpt … -count n` delivers the corpus to a named test account.
- **Runbook:** upgrades of clusters without `fts.shards_max` in their
  overlay (silent cap change 32 → 128), rolling update and way back with
  large dictionaries.

## Review

One review round by a second model on the code: a skewed account below
its cap would have received a no-op job every six hours, the upgrade
note for clusters without the overlay key, the difference between the
write-path trigger and the plan's sums, the cost of `fts stats -exact`,
a process-global gauge, line ends in `shardpost-load`; all fixed.

## Measurement for stage B14h (offline)

The decision of §7.4 was measured without any cluster: 36 246 messages of
the bench corpus per variant through the indexing text preparation,
unique terms and dictionary keys counted per account (no real mail
involved). Dictionary keys per message:

| Corpus | Today | N = 13 | N = 25 | N = 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ids` | 291.9 | 100.7 | 127.5 | 192.3 |
| `ids,nosig` (c3: no signature and trace headers of attached messages) | 199.9 | 87.1 | 99.7 | 146.7 |

Between 12 000 and 36 246 messages each new message adds 276 keys today
and 82 with N = 25 and c3. By the rule of the plan, N = 40 with c3 misses
the target of about 100 keys per message; **N = 25 with c3** reaches it.
The corpus is synthetic (calibrated on key counts only); the share of
attached headers in real mail can differ.
