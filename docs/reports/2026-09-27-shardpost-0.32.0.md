# ShardPost 0.32.0: JMAP principals, shareWith and share notifications (stage 6c-2)

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: rolled out, healthy; JMAP on the test endpoint
`jmap.roessner-net.de`.

## What the release contains

- **Principals (RFC 9670 2, `urn:ietf:params:jmap:principals`)**:
  - `Principal/get`, `/query`, `/changes`, `/queryChanges`. `Principal/set` refuses every change.
  - A principal id is the canonical account name.
  - Every owned account carries `urn:ietf:params:jmap:principals:owner`.
  - No directory listing. A user sees itself, the owners sharing with it, and the identifiers in its own ACLs.
  - Anything else must be named exactly: a `Principal/query` email condition, a `Principal/get` id or a `shareWith` key. That name is resolved through the Nauthilus identity lookup and counts only as the canonical account on this shard.
  - Limits:
    - `jmap.max_principal_lookups`: 5 per request.
    - `jmap.principal_miss_per_minute`: 30 lookups that find nothing, per account and across requests.
    - `jmap.max_principals`: 1000.
  - Every lookup passes the client address to Nauthilus. It is logged at Info with a hash prefix of the address and the result.
- **Mailbox sharing (draft-ietf-jmap-mail-sharing, `urn:ietf:params:jmap:mail:share`)**:
  - `myRights.mayShare` is IMAP `a`. `Mailbox.shareWith` maps the ACL to JMAP rights and is shown only to holders of `a`.
  - Writes need `a` and go through the IMAP SETACL store path.
  - Only the rights of properties whose value changes are touched, so `p` and half pairs survive.
  - The owner, `anyone` and negative identifiers are never shown or changed. All RFC 8620 patch forms work.
  - A creation may carry `shareWith`. If its ACL cannot be written, the mailbox is deleted again.
  - Without the new capabilities in `using`, Mailbox objects look as before.
- **Share notifications (RFC 9670 3)**:
  - A grant, change or revocation through JMAP, IMAP SETACL/DELETEACL or the admin API writes a notification for the grantee.
  - Coalescing:
    - A later change to the same mailbox replaces the notification and keeps the original old rights.
    - A change back to the start leaves none.
    - Changes to `anyone` and changes to one's own entry leave none.
  - Bounds:
    - `server.share_notifications_max`: 50, at most 200.
    - `server.share_notifications_retention_seconds`: 30 days.
  - Writing is best effort and never fails the ACL change.
  - `ShareNotification/get`, `/changes`, `/query` and `/set` (destroy only).

## Review

Two Fable rounds.

- **First round**: no blocker. One should-fix: the exact lookup was an address-existence oracle, bounded per request but not across requests, and invisible to Nauthilus. Fixed with:
  - the client address passed to Nauthilus;
  - the per-account miss budget;
  - lookup logging.
  
  A creation whose `shareWith` failed after the mailbox existed is now rolled back.
- **Second round**: confirmed both fixes. It checked that the bucket eviction cannot be abused to reset one's own budget, and that the rollback cannot delete a pre-existing mailbox.

Known limits, documented:
- A shared account is listed whether or not the user subscribes to one of its mailboxes. This deliberately differs from RFC 9670 1.4, to keep 6c-1 clients working.
- Notifications:
  - None is written for a grantee on another shard, for mailbox delete or rename, or for the account-purge jobs.
  - They are written synchronously in the ACL answer path.
- A `shareWith` update naming several principals is not atomic.
- An identifier already in one's own ACLs is accepted without a lookup, so a typo can spread.
- `Principal/changes` answers only for the current state, and both `/queryChanges` are `cannotCalculateChanges`.
- The miss buckets are per front-end process.
- Principals and notifications are not pushed.

## Live acceptance (public test endpoint, test accounts)

- `hack/jmap-acceptance-stage6c2.py` (`rbdpoc1` shares with `rbdpoc2`):
  - The session offers `principals` and `share`. `currentUserPrincipalId` is the login.
  - `Principal/query` with `rbdpoc2`'s exact address finds it, and a made-up address finds nothing. `Principal/get` returns an `individual` principal.
  - `Mailbox/set` creates a mailbox with a read-only `shareWith`. The owner sees it, with `mayShare` true.
  - For `rbdpoc2`:
    - The shared account appears. The mailbox is visible, `mayShare` is false and `shareWith` is null.
    - Trying to widen its own rights is `forbidden`.
  - The owner patches one right (`shareWith/<principal>/maySetKeywords`), and the answer carries the new `shareWith`.
  - The grantee has exactly one notification, with the widened rights and the owner as `changedBy`.
  - After the owner removes the share, the shared account is gone. The notification cancelled out, as RFC 9670 6.3 prescribes for a change back to the start.
  - The mailbox was destroyed.
- The server log shows the lookups at Info as `found`/`unknown` with the address hash only.
- **Regression:**
  - `hack/jmap-acceptance-stage6c.py` (IMAP SETACL sharing) passed; shared accounts now also carry `owner` and `share`.
  - `hack/jmap-acceptance.py`, `-stage6a.py` and `-stage6b.py` passed: sending and refusals, Quota, the vacation reply, Blob and SieveScript.
